1. TITLE OF INVENTION

"A self-powered in-tank water quality monitoring and automated sediment-flush system with closed-loop feedback for domestic and institutional water storage tanks"

2. FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of water storage tank maintenance and water quality management. More particularly, the invention pertains to an integrated, self-powered sensing and actuation unit that is removably installed within a domestic or institutional water storage tank (overhead or underground), continuously measures key water quality parameters of the stored water, computes a composite water quality score indicative of accumulated contamination, and automatically or semi-automatically actuates a bottom-mounted flush valve to selectively remove the settled sediment and particulate layer at the tank floor — without draining the tank — when the computed quality score indicates that settled contamination has exceeded a predetermined threshold.

3. BACKGROUND AND PROBLEM STATEMENT

3.1 The Forgotten Tank Problem

Water storage tanks in residential households, schools, hospitals, hostels, apartment complexes, and commercial buildings across India and similar tropical regions are among the most neglected components of the safe drinking water chain. Municipal or borewell water entering these tanks is typically treated and relatively clean at the point of entry. However, water that is stored in tanks for hours to weeks undergoes progressive degradation through the following mechanisms:

  1. Dissipation of disinfectant residual — Free chlorine, which inhibits microbial growth, dissipates within 24 to 48 hours of storage, removing the primary safeguard against bacterial proliferation.
  2. Sedimentation of suspended particles — Fine particulate matter (silt, clay, corrosion products, organic detritus) carried in by the source water settles under gravity onto the tank floor, forming a persistent sludge layer that is not removed by normal outlet flow.
  3. Biofilm formation — Microbial colonies colonize the submerged internal surfaces (walls, floor, baffles, inlet pipe exteriors), forming a slimy biofilm matrix that periodically sheds cells and fragments into the stored water.
  4. Algal growth — In transparent or semi-transparent tanks exposed to sunlight, algae proliferate on illuminated surfaces and in the water column, particularly during warm months.
  5. Chemical changes — pH may drift due to CO₂ absorption, pipe corrosion, or biological activity; dissolved solids may leach from accumulated debris.

3.2 The Detection Gap

Currently, tank owners detect water quality degradation through three unsatisfactory methods:

  1. Olfactory/gustatory detection — By the time the water smells "off" or tastes foul, significant microbial proliferation and/or biofilm shedding has already occurred. The consumer has already been exposed to degraded water.
  2. Visual detection — Discoloration or visible cloudiness indicates advanced contamination (turbidity typically exceeding 5 NTU), long after the degradation process began.
  3. Calendar-based cleaning — Tanks are cleaned on arbitrary schedules (monthly, quarterly, annually) regardless of actual contamination state, leading to either insufficient cleaning (health risk) or excessive cleaning (wasted water, labor, cost).

3.3 The Root Cause: Settled Sediment That No One Addresses

The fundamental physical reality that existing solutions fail to address is that the primary contamination reservoir in a stored-water tank is the settled sludge layer at the tank floor. This layer:

  • Accumulates continuously from incoming water particulates
  • Provides a nutrient-rich substrate for anaerobic bacterial growth
  • Re-suspends turbulently during inlet refilling, temporarily spiking water column turbidity
  • Is not removed by outlet draw-off (outlet is typically above the sludge layer)
  • Requires manual drain-and-scrub cleaning, which is labor-intensive, water-wasting, and infrequently performed

3.4 Limitations of Existing Approaches

Existing patents and products in this domain suffer from one or more of the following limitations:

  • Monitoring without actuation: Sensor-only systems (e.g., WO2024173389A1) detect water quality changes but provide no mechanism to remediate them; the user is merely alerted.
  • Full-tank drain cleaning: Automated cleaning systems (e.g., IN202441068267) use mechanical wipers and drain the entire tank, wasting large volumes of water and requiring the tank to be out of service.
  • Industrial-scale solutions: Sludge discharge systems (e.g., CN223988161U) are designed for large municipal sedimentation tanks using camera-based image analysis, not for domestic or institutional storage tanks.
  • In-pipe / point-of-use monitoring: Systems like WO2024173389A1 and Delta Faucet's application monitor water in pipes or at faucets, not in the storage tank where degradation actually occurs.
  • Chemical treatment approaches: Systems that dispense chemicals (e.g., chlorine) address microbial contamination but do not remove the physical sludge reservoir that sustains microbial growth.
  • Cloud dependence: Most IoT-based approaches require internet connectivity and cloud services, adding subscription cost and privacy concerns.

4. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

4.1 Core Inventive Concept

The present invention provides a single integrated unit that combines continuous in-tank water quality sensing, composite contamination scoring with trend prediction, and a targeted bottom-flush actuation mechanism, all powered by an onboard solar-battery system, and operating entirely locally without cloud dependency.

The key inventive step is the closed-loop integration of sensing and targeted sediment-flush actuation:

  1. A multi-parameter sensor array submerged in the stored water continuously measures turbidity, pH, total dissolved solids (TDS), temperature, and water level.
  2. An onboard processor computes a composite TankScore — a single weighted, trend-aware index that quantifies the overall contamination state of the stored water, with particular emphasis on indicators of settled-sediment activity (turbidity trend, TDS leaching rate, refill-induced resuspension events).
  3. When the TankScore crosses a predetermined "clean-required" threshold (or when a manual trigger is received from a connected user interface), the processor actuates a normally-closed, bottom-mounted flush valve via a low-side or high-side switch, causing the valve to open for a predetermined duration.
  4. During the flush interval, the high-concentration sediment layer at the tank floor is selectively drained through the flush valve — without draining the bulk water column above. The valve is positioned at the lowest point of the tank to preferentially discharge the highest-turbidity, highest-TDS water.
  5. After the flush interval, the sensor array re-measures water quality and computes a post-flush TankScore, verifying the effectiveness of the flush. This closed-loop verification step is a critical aspect of the invention — the system confirms whether the flush actually improved water quality, and if not, may trigger additional flush cycles or escalate the alert to require manual cleaning.
  6. A complete event log (pre-flush score, flush duration, post-flush score, effectiveness ratio) is stored locally and presented on a user-accessible dashboard, enabling the tank owner to track long-term contamination trends and cleaning effectiveness.

4.2 Novel Aspects (Distinguishing from Prior Art)

1. Closed-loop flush verificationSystem measures water quality AFTER flushing and compares with pre-flush quality to compute a quantitative effectiveness score. This feedback loop is absent in all surveyed prior art.

The flush duration and valve bore are selected so that only the bottom 5–15% of tank volume is discharged. This is fundamentally different from full-tank drain-and-clean systems.

Sludge-resuspension event detectionThe system distinguishes between (a) gradual degradation (biofilm growth), (b) sudden contamination from source water (refill events), and (c) sludge resuspension (inlet turbulence). This is a deliberate architectural choice that distinguishes from all IoT-based prior art which requires cloud platforms.

5.3 TankScore Computation Engine

The TankScore is a composite numerical index (0–100) computed by the onboard processor. It integrates three classes of information:

(a) Instantaneous Parameter Sub-Scores

Each sensor parameter is converted to a 0–100 sub-score based on its distance from a safe reference band. For example, turbidity below 1 NTU scores 100; between 1–5 NTU scores linearly from 100 to 50; above 5 NTU scores 0.

(b) Trend Analysis

Linear regression is performed on the last 14 days (112 readings at 3-hour intervals) of turbidity, TDS, and pH data. The slope (rate of change) of each parameter contributes a trend bonus or penalty to the TankScore. A rising turbidity trend triggers a premature WATCH or CLEAN-NOW status even before the absolute threshold is breached, providing predictive capability.

(c) Event Classification

The system classifies water quality events into three categories based on correlated multi-parameter signatures:

  • Gradual degradation: Turbidity slowly rising with stable pH → biofilm accumulation. TankScore penalized proportionally to slope.
  • Source water contamination: Level rise (refill detected) followed by turbidity spike → contaminated incoming water. TankScore penalized based on peak turbidity but trend analysis is suppressed for settling period.
  • Sludge resuspension: Turbidity spike without level change (inlet turbulence, temperature convection) → resuspension of existing bottom layer. TankScore penalized and flush recommendation issued (this is the primary flush trigger).

5.4 Automated Sediment-Flush Actuation Subsystem

The flush subsystem comprises:

  1. Flush valve: A normally-closed, electrically actuated valve (solenoid or motorized ball valve) with a bore diameter selected for the tank size (typically ½ inch to 1 inch for domestic tanks). The valve is installed at the lowest drain point of the tank via a T-fitting or existing drain tap. The valve body material is selected for potable water contact (brass with food-grade EPDM seal, or SS316).
  2. Valve driver: A MOSFET-based low-side switch driven by a GPIO output of the processor, with flyback protection (diode or RC snubber) for inductive loads (solenoid coils). The driver circuit is housed in the main enclosure and connected to the valve by a waterproof cable.
  3. Flush duration controller: The processor controls the valve open duration based on (a) tank volume (derived from level sensor), (b) pre-flush turbidity level, and (c) user-configurable sludge thickness parameter. Typical flush durations range from 30 seconds to 120 seconds for domestic tanks.
  4. Post-flush verification: After the flush interval, the system waits a predetermined settling time (typically 15–60 minutes), re-measures all sensor parameters, and computes the post-flush TankScore. The flush effectiveness is calculated as:

Flush Effectiveness (%) = (Pre-flush Turbidity − Post-flush Turbidity) / Pre-flush Turbidity × 100

If effectiveness exceeds a predetermined threshold (e.g., 40%), the flush is confirmed successful and the TankScore is updated. If effectiveness is below threshold, the system may issue a second flush command or escalate to a manual-cleaning-required alert.

5.5 Power Management Subsystem

The unit is self-powered by a solar photovoltaic panel and rechargeable battery system:

  • Solar panel: 5–10 W, 12 V nominal, mounted adjacent to or integral with the enclosure.
  • Charge controller: MPPT or PWM, with overcharge and over-discharge protection.
  • Battery: Lithium-ion (18650 or LiFePO₄) pack, capacity selected for 2–3 weeks of zero-sun autonomy.
  • Regulated power rails: 12 V bus for flush valve actuation; 5 V rail for sensor excitation; 3.3 V rail for processor and digital circuits.
  • Duty cycling: The processor enters deep-sleep mode between measurement cycles, waking at predetermined intervals (default: every 3 hours) to power the sensor rail, perform measurements, compute TankScore, evaluate flush need, and serve any pending user requests. Deep-sleep current is minimized (<1 mA) to maximize battery life.

5.6 User Interface Subsystem

The processor hosts a local web server accessible over the tank's WiFi network (no internet required). The interface provides:

  • Real-time gauge display of all measured parameters
  • TankScore status banner with color-coded indication
  • Trend charts (24-hour, 7-day, 30-day, 90-day) for each parameter
  • "Flush Now" button for manual override of automated flush
  • "Tank Cleaned" confirmation button (resets the cleaning interval counter and logs the event)
  • Flush history log (timestamp, pre-flush score, post-flush score, effectiveness)
  • Battery and solar charging status
  • Calibration interface for sensor maintenance

6. INDEPENDENT CLAIM (DRAFT)

CLAIM 1 (Independent — System Claim):

A self-powered in-tank water quality monitoring and automated sediment-flush system for a water storage tank, the system comprising:

(a) an enclosure configured to be removably mounted on or within said water storage tank;

(b) a plurality of water quality sensors suspended from said enclosure and submerged in stored water within said tank, said sensors comprising at least a turbidity sensor, a total dissolved solids sensor, a pH sensor, a temperature sensor, and a water level sensor;

(c) a processor disposed within said enclosure, operatively connected to said sensors, configured to:

(i) periodically measure water quality parameters from said sensors;

(ii) compute a composite water quality score from said measured parameters, said score incorporating at least an instantaneous parameter component and a trend component derived from rate-of-change of said parameters over a predetermined historical window;

(iii) classify said composite score into one of a plurality of quality states; and

(iv) generate a flush actuation signal when said composite score indicates a quality state requiring sediment removal;

(d) a normally-closed flush valve installed at a lowest drainage point of said tank and fluidly connected to said enclosure by a waterproof cable, said flush valve being configured to open upon receipt of said flush actuation signal and to discharge a sediment-rich bottom layer of said stored water through a drain outlet, without draining a bulk water column above said bottom layer;

(e) a valve driver circuit disposed within said enclosure, operatively connecting said processor to said flush valve, said driver circuit configured to switch electrical power to said flush valve in response to said flush actuation signal;

(f) a solar photovoltaic panel and rechargeable battery disposed within or adjacent to said enclosure, said solar panel and battery providing electrical power to said sensors, said processor, and said flush valve; and

(g) a local wireless network interface hosted by said processor, configured to serve a user dashboard to a client device on a same local network without requiring internet connectivity,

wherein said processor is further configured to re-measure said water quality parameters after said flush valve is closed, compute a post-flush composite water quality score, compare said post-flush score with said pre-flush score to derive a flush effectiveness metric, and store said flush effectiveness metric in a local event log for presentation on said user dashboard.

9. PATENTABILITY ASSESSMENT

Novelty (Section 32 of Indian Patents Act, 1970): The claimed subject matter is not anticipated by any single prior art reference found in the preliminary search. The combination of in-tank multi-parameter sensing, trend-predictive composite scoring, selective bottom-flush actuation, and closed-loop flush verification in a single self-powered unit is not disclosed in any surveyed reference.

Inventive Step / Non-Obviousness: While each individual component (sensors, solenoid valve, ESP32, solar panel, web server) is known in isolation, their specific combination and integration into a closed-loop system that (a) distinguishes settled-sediment contamination from other contamination sources, (b) triggers a selective partial flush (not full drain), and (c) verifies flush effectiveness by re-measurement, is not obvious to a person skilled in the art of water tank maintenance or IoT sensor systems. The closed-loop verification step in particular represents a non-trivial technical contribution that addresses a real problem (silent flush failure due to valve clogging or insufficient duration) that is not recognized in prior art.

Industrial Applicability: The invention is directly applicable to millions of domestic and institutional water storage tanks in India and similar regions. The system uses commercially available components and can be manufactured at low cost. The self-powered, cloud-free design makes it suitable for deployment in areas with unreliable electricity and internet connectivity.
