1. Title of Invention

A Self-Powered IoT System for In-Tank Water Quality Monitoring and Safe Pump Control for Domestic and Institutional Water Storage Tanks.

Full title: "A self-powered IoT-based in-tank water quality monitoring system with edge-computed composite scoring, trend prediction and a local-network dashboard, paired with a mains-isolated home pump controller for domestic and institutional water storage tanks."

2. Abstract

Water storage tanks in homes, schools, hospitals and apartment buildings degrade silently: disinfectant residual dissipates within 24–48 hours, suspended particles settle into a sludge layer at the tank floor, biofilm forms on internal surfaces, and algae proliferate in warm conditions. By the time degradation is detected by smell, taste or sight, the water is already unsafe. Existing solutions either monitor without acting, or clean by draining and scrubbing the entire tank — wasting water and labour.

TankGuard is a three-unit system. The tank unit M1 is self-powered, mounted on the lid, and continuously measures five water-quality parameters — turbidity, TDS, pH, temperature and water level — through submerged probes. An on-device ESP8266 performs all computation at the network edge: it fuses the readings into a weighted composite TankScore, analyses 14-day trends, and classifies the contamination event (gradual biofilm growth, source-water contamination, or sludge resuspension). When the score indicates that the water needs attention, the system raises a local alert and can switch the pump through a separate home unit using an opto-isolated relay and a motor-rated contactor — the ESP8266 never touches mains. A crash or lost link forces the pump OFF. All logic runs locally with no cloud, subscription or internet requirement; a Wi-Fi access point and embedded web server present an interactive dashboard to any phone or browser on the local network. The tank unit is powered by a small solar panel and battery, with duty-cycled sensing for multi-week autonomy.

4. Features

  • Multi-parameter sensing — turbidity, TDS, pH, temperature and water level measured continuously in-tank
  • TankScore composite index — one 0–100 number answering "is it time to clean?"
  • Trend-predictive analysis — 14-day linear regression detects degradation before visible symptoms
  • Event classification — distinguishes biofilm growth, source-water contamination and sludge resuspension
  • Mains-safe pump control — opto-relay → contactor → pump, with fail-safe OFF
  • Manual override — one physical button plus dashboard "Tank Cleaned" confirmation
  • Solar-powered autonomy — runs off-grid for weeks without sun
  • Cloud-free local dashboard — any phone/browser, no internet, no account
  • Edge data logging — full history stored on-device; optional OTA updates and alert exports

5. What Makes the System Unique

The invention is distinguished from prior art (sensor-only monitors, full-tank drain cleaners, industrial camera-based sludge systems, in-pipe point-of-use devices) by the following non-obvious combinations:

Unique AspectDistinguishing Feature
1. Trend-predictive TankScoreTriggering uses a weighted composite with 14-day slope and event classification, not a single-sensor threshold — enabling action before visible degradation.
2. Sludge-resuspension detectionCorrelating turbidity spikes against level changes distinguishes resuspension from source contamination — a specific diagnosis absent from prior art.
3. Edge-only IoT architectureAll sensing, scoring, decision and alerting execute on-device; the dashboard is self-hosted over the local network. No cloud, subscription or account — a deliberate architectural contrast to all surveyed IoT monitors.
4. Duty-cycled solar autonomyDeep-sleep MCU with switched sensor power rails achieves multi-week off-grid operation while maintaining continuous logical monitoring.
5. Local three-unit architectureSensing, edge computing, pump control and cleaning share one local network with no cloud service. The tank unit needs no tank penetration, and the pump unit stays isolated from mains.

Fig. 1 — Four functional layers plus power; all computation stays at Layer 4 (edge).

Fig. 2 — Operating Cycle (sense → compute → actuate → verify)

Step 1 — Wake: RTC timer wakes the ESP8266 (every 3 h); sensor power rail energised.
Step 2 — Acquire: Read turbidity, TDS, pH, temperature, level; median-filter each.
Step 3 — Score: Normalise + weight parameters into TankScore; append sample to 14-day buffer.
Step 4 — Analyse: 14-day regression + classify event (degradation / source contamination / sludge resuspension).
Step 5 — Decide: Map the score to GOOD, WATCH or ATTENTION; raise a local alert.
Step 6 — Control: Home unit runs the pump via opto-relay → contactor, or leaves it OFF; fail-safe OFF on crash or link loss.
Step 7 — Report: Log event; update local dashboard (readings, TankScore, trends, pump status, battery).
Step 8 — Sleep: De-energise sensors; deep sleep <1 mA until next cycle.
